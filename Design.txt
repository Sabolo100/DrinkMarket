Az alábbi promptot úgy érdemes használni, hogy ne egyszerűen „szép weboldalt” kérjen, hanem egyszerre adjon kreatív rendezői, UX/UI, motion, technikai és QA feladatot. Beépítettem azt is, hogy kerülje a tipikus AI-generált webdesign kliséket, és egyetlen erős, következetes vizuális koncepció köré építse az oldalt.

# MASTER PROMPT — DESIGN AND BUILD A WORLD-CLASS, DISTINCTIVE WEBSITE

You are simultaneously acting as:

* an award-winning Digital Creative Director,
* a world-class UX/UI Designer,
* an elite Creative Technologist,
* a senior Web Motion Designer,
* a senior Front-End Engineer,
* a Conversion and Brand Strategist,
* and a meticulous Creative QA Director.

Your task is to transform **[BRAND / COMPANY / PRODUCT]** into a genuinely distinctive, premium, modern website that feels as if it were conceived and crafted by one of the world’s best independent digital studios.

The website must NOT feel like a template, a generic SaaS landing page, a standard Webflow/Framer site, or an obviously AI-generated design.

The final result should have a clear creative idea, a recognizable visual identity, excellent typography, carefully designed layouts, meaningful interaction, sophisticated motion, strong storytelling, and flawless execution.

The goal is not to add as many effects as possible.

The goal is to create **one coherent, memorable digital experience where concept, visual design, storytelling, interaction and technology reinforce each other.**

---

# 1. UNDERSTAND THE BRAND FIRST

Before designing anything, study all supplied information about:

* the company,
* its products and services,
* target audiences,
* competitors,
* positioning,
* business goals,
* tone of voice,
* brand personality,
* existing visual identity,
* available imagery,
* content,
* references,
* and technical constraints.

Identify:

1. What is genuinely distinctive about this brand?
2. What should a visitor understand within the first 5 seconds?
3. What should they remember after leaving?
4. What emotional impression should the website create?
5. What is the main conversion goal?
6. What visual or interactive metaphor could uniquely represent the brand?

Do NOT start by choosing a standard website layout.

First define the **central creative concept**.

The entire site should feel as if it grew from this idea.

---

# 2. CREATE A HIGH-END CREATIVE DIRECTION

Develop a complete creative direction for the website.

Define:

* overall visual concept,
* mood,
* visual personality,
* art direction,
* typography,
* color system,
* spacing system,
* grid,
* layout philosophy,
* image treatment,
* illustration style,
* 3D language if appropriate,
* iconography,
* graphic motifs,
* surfaces,
* borders,
* depth,
* lighting,
* texture,
* motion philosophy,
* and interaction language.

The design must feel:

* custom,
* intentional,
* contemporary,
* sophisticated,
* confident,
* technically advanced,
* visually memorable,
* and appropriate for the brand.

Avoid design choices simply because they are currently fashionable.

Every major stylistic choice should support the brand story.

---

# 3. CREATE ONE STRONG SIGNATURE VISUAL IDEA

The website needs a recognizable **signature visual system**.

Find one central visual metaphor, transformation, spatial idea, object, system, animation or graphic principle that can recur throughout the experience.

Examples of possible approaches — only use these if genuinely relevant:

* evolution,
* transformation,
* expanding capability,
* changing states,
* modular systems,
* networks,
* layers,
* physical-to-digital transitions,
* progressive complexity,
* adaptive structures,
* spatial environments,
* data becoming form,
* fragmented elements becoming complete,
* controlled generative systems.

Do not combine many unrelated visual metaphors.

Choose the strongest one and develop it deeply.

This signature idea should influence:

* hero,
* section transitions,
* graphics,
* diagrams,
* interactions,
* imagery,
* animations,
* navigation details,
* and possibly the loading experience.

---

# 4. DESIGN A SIGNATURE HERO

Create a hero section that is immediately recognizable and could not simply be reused for another company.

The hero must communicate the brand’s core proposition within seconds.

Design:

* headline hierarchy,
* supporting copy,
* CTA structure,
* composition,
* visual focal point,
* imagery / animation / 3D / generative graphics,
* spatial depth,
* lighting,
* background,
* foreground elements,
* interaction,
* cursor response,
* entrance sequence,
* and transition into the next section.

The visual concept should dominate the hero, not decorative UI.

Avoid the standard formula:

headline + paragraph + two buttons + dashboard mockup.

Instead create a real composition.

The hero should feel almost like the opening scene of a carefully directed digital experience.

However, clarity must remain stronger than spectacle.

---

# 5. DESIGN THE COMPLETE WEBSITE JOURNEY

Design the whole website as one continuous narrative.

Map the optimal page structure based on the actual business goals rather than following a generic landing-page formula.

Consider sections such as:

* navigation,
* hero,
* brand proposition,
* problem/context,
* core services or products,
* differentiators,
* methodology,
* capabilities,
* case studies,
* results,
* social proof,
* technology,
* process,
* team,
* credibility,
* insights,
* conversion sections,
* forms,
* footer.

Only include sections that genuinely serve the user journey.

For EVERY section define:

* its objective,
* primary message,
* content hierarchy,
* layout,
* visual composition,
* interaction,
* animation,
* background treatment,
* relationship to surrounding sections,
* and transition into the next section.

Avoid repetitive stacks of identical cards.

Create visual rhythm.

Alternate between:

* dense and spacious,
* large and intimate,
* static and interactive,
* dark and light if appropriate,
* text-driven and visual,
* structured and expressive.

The page should feel directed rather than assembled.

---

# 6. CREATE A STRONG LAYOUT SYSTEM

Do not simply place everything inside centered 1200px containers.

Use sophisticated editorial composition.

Explore where appropriate:

* asymmetrical grids,
* deliberate oversized whitespace,
* off-grid typography,
* large-scale typography,
* overlapping elements,
* controlled cropping,
* horizontal movement,
* vertical narratives,
* split compositions,
* sticky sections,
* progressive disclosure,
* layered sections,
* scrolling visual sequences.

Maintain usability and responsive logic.

Every unusual composition must have a reason.

---

# 7. TYPOGRAPHY MUST BE A DESIGN ELEMENT

Typography must carry a significant part of the visual identity.

Define:

* primary display typeface,
* body typeface,
* secondary styles if needed,
* type scale,
* weights,
* line heights,
* tracking,
* paragraph width,
* heading behavior,
* responsive typography.

Use typography compositionally.

Large type may become part of the visual environment.

Avoid the typical AI website look where:

* every headline is centered,
* every heading has a gradient,
* every paragraph is the same width,
* every section follows the same typography hierarchy.

Create intentional variation while maintaining consistency.

---

# 8. BUILD A PREMIUM MOTION SYSTEM

Motion must feel integrated into the design rather than added afterward.

Define a unified animation language.

Include only meaningful effects such as:

* page-load choreography,
* hero entrance animation,
* scroll-triggered reveals,
* text reveals,
* masking,
* image transitions,
* parallax,
* depth shifts,
* sticky sequences,
* section transformations,
* hover states,
* navigation transitions,
* button responses,
* cursor interactions,
* micro-interactions,
* page transitions.

Animations must feel:

* smooth,
* restrained,
* responsive,
* physically believable,
* consistent.

Use carefully designed easing rather than generic linear movement.

Create hierarchy in motion:

* micro animations: ~150–300 ms,
* interface transitions: ~300–600 ms,
* major storytelling transitions: ~600–1200 ms where appropriate.

Do not animate everything.

Static moments are essential for visual rhythm.

Respect:

`prefers-reduced-motion`.

---

# 9. DESIGN SCROLL AS STORYTELLING

Scrolling should reveal the website progressively.

Where relevant, use scroll position to create meaningful transitions:

* systems assembling,
* objects transforming,
* layers separating,
* content progressing,
* capability increasing,
* visual states changing,
* data evolving,
* camera depth shifting.

Avoid pointless scroll-jacking.

Native scrolling should remain natural and predictable.

The user should feel that the page is responding to their movement, not fighting it.

---

# 10. CREATE SOPHISTICATED INTERACTIONS

Design meaningful interaction states for:

* navigation,
* buttons,
* links,
* cards,
* media,
* case studies,
* filters,
* accordions,
* forms,
* CTAs,
* galleries,
* draggable content if applicable.

Hover states should reveal information or reinforce the visual language.

Avoid generic effects such as simply scaling every card to 1.05.

Interaction should add character.

---

# 11. AVOID THE GENERIC “AI WEBSITE” LOOK

Actively identify and remove anything that makes the website look templated or AI-generated.

Avoid overusing:

* gradient blobs,
* glowing purple backgrounds,
* generic glassmorphism,
* endless rounded cards,
* floating pills,
* icon + title + paragraph feature grids,
* excessive border-radius,
* random neon glows,
* decorative particles without meaning,
* fake dashboards,
* meaningless 3D spheres,
* stock illustrations,
* generic business photography,
* centered-everything layouts,
* repeated three-column sections,
* identical section spacing,
* excessive badges,
* excessive gradients,
* unnecessary animated backgrounds.

If using any of these techniques, they must have a clear conceptual reason.

Always ask:

**Could this design belong to another company with only the logo changed?**

If yes, redesign it.

---

# 12. MAKE IT FEEL AGENCY-BUILT

Continuously audit the site for anything that feels:

* cheap,
* generic,
* inconsistent,
* unfinished,
* auto-generated,
* overly symmetrical,
* visually flat,
* overdecorated,
* or technically amateur.

Pay special attention to:

* typography,
* spacing,
* content width,
* line breaks,
* visual hierarchy,
* image crops,
* alignment,
* borders,
* shadows,
* button proportions,
* hover details,
* animation timing,
* transitions,
* empty states,
* form fields,
* mobile layout,
* navigation.

Add small but deliberate details that make the experience feel authored.

Every pixel does not need decoration.

Every pixel needs intention.

---

# 13. DESIGN MOBILE AS A SEPARATE EXPERIENCE

Do not merely shrink the desktop design.

Create a proper mobile interpretation.

Reconsider:

* hierarchy,
* spacing,
* typography,
* image crops,
* interactions,
* navigation,
* sticky elements,
* animation,
* content order,
* touch areas,
* horizontal layouts,
* immersive visuals.

Maintain the same creative concept while adapting it intelligently to small screens.

Avoid excessive scroll length created by stacking every desktop element vertically.

---

# 14. CONVERSION MUST REMAIN CLEAR

The site can be visually ambitious, but users must always understand:

* who the company is,
* what it offers,
* why it is different,
* why they should trust it,
* what they should do next.

Define a clear CTA hierarchy.

Primary CTA should be visually recognizable throughout the site without becoming repetitive.

Conversion sections should feel like part of the storytelling rather than generic marketing blocks.

---

# 15. IMPLEMENT WITH A PROFESSIONAL FRONT-END ARCHITECTURE

Use a modern, production-ready architecture appropriate for the project.

Prefer reusable components without allowing the component system to dictate generic design.

Before implementing anything:

1. inspect the existing project,
2. understand its structure,
3. identify working functionality,
4. preserve existing features unless explicitly replacing them.

When modifying an existing website:

* never blindly rewrite the application,
* avoid breaking working functionality,
* inspect dependencies first,
* reuse existing components when sensible,
* refactor only where beneficial.

Build the site incrementally.

After every significant change:

1. inspect the result,
2. test it,
3. fix visual or functional problems,
4. verify responsiveness,
5. ensure previous functionality still works.

---

# 16. PERFORMANCE IS PART OF THE DESIGN

The website must feel extremely smooth.

Optimize:

* image formats,
* image loading,
* fonts,
* animation workload,
* JavaScript,
* DOM complexity,
* GPU usage,
* 3D rendering,
* lazy loading,
* bundle size,
* responsive assets.

Prefer transforms and opacity for animation.

Avoid unnecessary layout recalculations.

If using WebGL / Three.js / shaders:

* use them because they strengthen the concept,
* keep geometry efficient,
* limit draw calls,
* optimize textures,
* provide fallbacks,
* ensure acceptable mobile performance.

Visual ambition must never make the website feel sluggish.

---

# 17. ACCESSIBILITY

Maintain strong accessibility without compromising the visual concept.

Ensure:

* semantic HTML,
* keyboard navigation,
* logical tab order,
* visible focus states,
* sufficient contrast,
* accessible forms,
* proper labels,
* meaningful alt text,
* ARIA only where necessary,
* reduced-motion support.

Interactive visual effects must never block basic navigation.

---

# 18. SEO AND TECHNICAL QUALITY

Implement:

* correct page titles,
* meta descriptions,
* canonical structure,
* OpenGraph data,
* social sharing metadata,
* semantic headings,
* structured content,
* crawlable text,
* sitemap where appropriate,
* robots configuration,
* structured data where useful.

Ensure that visual creativity does not hide important content from search engines.

---

# 19. FINAL CREATIVE AUDIT

Before considering the site complete, perform a creative-director audit.

Ask:

* Is there one clear visual idea?
* Is the hero unforgettable?
* Does the website look like this specific brand?
* Is the typography strong enough?
* Is the layout too predictable?
* Are there too many cards?
* Are sections visually repetitive?
* Is motion meaningful?
* Are transitions smooth?
* Does anything feel like a template?
* Does anything feel AI-generated?
* Is there a strong visual rhythm?
* Does mobile feel intentionally designed?
* Are CTAs clear?
* Is the brand proposition immediately understandable?

If any answer is weak, improve the site.

---

Perform a full QA pass covering:

### Visual

* spacing,
* alignment,
* typography,
* consistency,
* imagery,
* responsive behavior.

### Interaction

* navigation,
* links,
* buttons,
* hover states,
* forms,
* scroll behavior,
* touch interactions.

### Motion

* FPS,
* timing,
* easing,
* scroll synchronization,
* reduced motion.

### Responsive

Test at minimum:

* 320 px,
* 375 px,
* 430 px,
* 768 px,
* 1024 px,
* 1280 px,
* 1440 px,
* 1920 px.

### Browser

Check:

* Chrome,
* Safari,
* Firefox,
* Edge.

### Performance

Review:

* loading speed,
* Largest Contentful Paint,
* Cumulative Layout Shift,
* Interaction to Next Paint,
* image optimization,
* script size.

### Accessibility

Check:

* keyboard navigation,
* focus states,
* contrast,
* semantic structure,
* forms.

### SEO

Check:

* metadata,
* heading hierarchy,
* indexability,
* sharing metadata.

Rank all remaining problems:

* P0 — launch blocker
* P1 — major
* P2 — polish
* P3 — optional enhancement

Fix all P0 and P1 issues before considering the site complete.

---

# 21. WORKING METHOD

Do not attempt to generate the entire project blindly in one pass.

Work like a senior design and engineering team.

Use this sequence:

### Phase 1 — Discovery

Understand brand, audience, content and objectives.

### Phase 2 — Creative Concept

Define one distinctive creative direction and signature visual idea.

### Phase 3 — UX Architecture

Define navigation, page structure and conversion flow.

### Phase 4 — Visual System

Typography, grid, colors, imagery, graphic language.

### Phase 5 — Hero

Create and perfect the signature opening experience.

### Phase 6 — Core Sections

Build the storytelling system and visual rhythm.

### Phase 7 — Interaction & Motion

Add the unified interaction and animation language.

### Phase 8 — Responsive Design

Design and optimize mobile and tablet experiences.

### Phase 9 — Technical Optimization

Performance, accessibility and SEO.

### Phase 10 — Creative Polish

Remove anything generic or unfinished.

### Phase 11 — Final QA

Test everything and fix remaining issues.

At every phase, inspect the work already completed before making changes.

Never destroy working functionality unnecessarily.

---

# 22. QUALITY BAR

The target quality is NOT:

“A very good AI-generated website.”

The target is:

**A website that could credibly be featured by Awwwards, CSS Design Awards or a leading digital design publication while still functioning as a commercially effective business website.**

Do not imitate an existing award-winning website.

Learn from the level of:

* art direction,
* restraint,
* craftsmanship,
* typography,
* storytelling,
* motion,
* and detail.

The result must remain original to **[BRAND]**.

# FINAL INSTRUCTION

Do not settle for the first acceptable design.

Whenever a section looks conventional, ask whether it can be made more specific to the brand.

Whenever an effect exists only because it looks impressive, remove it.

Whenever functionality and visual ambition conflict, find an elegant solution rather than sacrificing either.

Create a website with:

**ONE IDEA.
ONE SYSTEM.
ONE VISUAL LANGUAGE.
EXCEPTIONAL EXECUTION.**

The final experience should feel unmistakably designed for **[BRAND]** and impossible to confuse with a generic template.

Ennek szerintem az egyik legfontosabb része az, hogy nem azt mondja a Claude-nak vagy más vibe coding rendszernek, hogy „legyen Awwwards-szerű”, hanem konkrétan előírja a **ONE IDEA → ONE SYSTEM → ONE VISUAL LANGUAGE** gondolkodást. Ettől sokkal kisebb az esélye annak, hogy kapsz egy szép, de teljesen csereszabatos „sötét háttér + lila glow + 12 rounded card” oldalt.

Én még egy dolgot tennék hozzá használatkor: a `[PROJECT INPUT]` részt minden konkrét projekthez töltsd ki rendesen, és mellé adj 3–6 olyan referenciaoldalt, ahol **külön leírod, hogy melyikből pontosan mi tetszik**. Így nem stílusmásolást kérsz, hanem kreatív alapanyagokat adsz.
